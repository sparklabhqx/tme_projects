About reaction time

A visual reaction is a relay race: photoreceptors fire (~20 ms), the signal reaches visual cortex (~50–80 ms), the brain decides, motor cortex fires, and the command travels down your arm to the finger (~20–30 ms). That pipeline puts a hard floor around 150 ms for visual stimuli — anything faster on this game was a guess, not a reaction.

An F1 start is around 200 ms from lights-out to moving; in athletics anything under 100 ms off the gun counts as a false start. Reaction time is trainable to a point, worsens with fatigue and age, and sound beats sight — auditory reactions run ~30–50 ms faster than visual ones.
